In Ontario, every Grade 5 student learns mathematics across six strands. Strand A runs through all the others — it is about the social-emotional learning skills and the mathematical processes (problem solving, reasoning, reflecting, connecting, communicating, representing, and selecting tools and strategies) that students use while doing math. Strands B–F describe the content learned this year.

A

Social-Emotional Learning Skills & Mathematical Processes

A1. Apply, to the best of their ability, a variety of social-emotional learning skills to support their use of the mathematical processes and their learning in mathematics.

  • Problem solving
  • Reasoning & proving
  • Reflecting
  • Connecting
  • Communicating
  • Representing
  • Selecting tools & strategies
B

Number

B1 · Number Sense

Demonstrate an understanding of numbers and make connections to the way numbers are used in everyday life.

  • Read, represent, compose, and decompose whole numbers up to 100 000.
  • Read, represent, compose, and decompose decimal numbers to hundredths.
  • Round decimal numbers to the nearest tenth and whole number.
  • Compare and order fractions; find and use equivalent fractions.
  • Describe relationships and equivalences among fractions and decimals to hundredths.

B2 · Operations

Use knowledge of numbers and operations to solve problems encountered in everyday life.

  • Use the properties of operations and the order of operations.
  • Recall and demonstrate multiplication facts to 12 × 12 and related division facts.
  • Multiply two- and three-digit whole numbers by one- and two-digit numbers; divide three-digit numbers by one- and two-digit numbers.
  • Add and subtract decimal numbers to hundredths.
  • Multiply and divide decimal tenths by whole numbers.
  • Add and subtract fractions with like denominators.
C

Algebra

C1 · Patterns and Relationships

Identify, describe, extend, create, and make predictions about a variety of patterns, including those found in real-life contexts.

  • Identify and describe increasing, decreasing, and repeating patterns and their pattern rules.
  • Represent patterns with tables of values and make predictions.

C2 · Equations and Inequalities

Demonstrate an understanding of variables, expressions, equalities, and inequalities, and apply this understanding in various contexts.

  • Use variables in expressions and equations.
  • Solve for unknown values in equations; work with equalities and inequalities.

C3 · Coding

Solve problems and create computational representations of mathematical situations using coding concepts and skills.

  • Write and execute code with sequential, concurrent, repeating, and nested events.
  • Read and alter existing code, and describe how changes affect outcomes.

C4 · Mathematical Modelling

Apply the process of mathematical modelling to represent, analyse, make predictions, and provide insight into real-life situations.

D

Data

D1 · Data Literacy

Manage, analyse, and use data to make convincing arguments and informed decisions, in various real-life contexts.

  • Collect data and organize it in frequency tables.
  • Determine the mean and identify the mode of a data set.
  • Create and interpret stem-and-leaf plots and double bar graphs.

D2 · Probability

Describe the likelihood that events will happen, and use that information to make predictions.

  • Use mathematical language (e.g., likely, unlikely, certain) to describe likelihood.
  • Compare experimental and theoretical probability.
E

Spatial Sense

E1 · Geometric and Spatial Reasoning

Describe and represent shape, location, and movement by applying geometric properties and spatial relationships.

  • Identify and classify quadrilaterals by their properties.
  • Construct three-dimensional objects and explore their nets.
  • Describe location and movement on a grid using coordinates and translations.

E2 · Measurement

Compare, estimate, and determine measurements in various contexts.

  • Convert and work with metric units of length, mass, and capacity.
  • Measure and construct angles using a protractor.
  • Determine the area of rectangles and the relationship between area and perimeter.
  • Explore the relationship between capacity and volume.
F

Financial Literacy

F1 · Money and Finances

Demonstrate the knowledge and skills needed to make informed financial decisions.

  • Describe methods of payment (cash, debit, credit) and estimate and calculate costs.
  • Make change and compare the value of purchases.
  • Set simple financial goals and describe how to reach them.
